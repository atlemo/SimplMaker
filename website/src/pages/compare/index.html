vs Fusion

Fusion is the benchmark: assemblies, CAM, simulation, rendering, electronics. It's free for qualifying hobbyists (under $1,000 in annual revenue, non-commercial), with a reduced feature set. Full use is a subscription of about $680 a year in the US.

SimplMaker does a fraction of what Fusion does, but it does the modelling-for-printing part without a subscription, an Autodesk account, or cloud storage.

Pick Fusion ifyou need assemblies, CAM or simulation, or you work on Windows.

vs FreeCAD

FreeCAD is free, open source and genuinely capable, with a native Apple Silicon build since 1.1. It is also a big toolbox of workbenches, and it takes time to learn.

SimplMaker is one focused workflow in a native Mac app: sketch, constrain, extrude, fillet, export. If FreeCAD ever felt like too much, this is the smaller version of the idea.

Pick FreeCAD iffree matters most, you want the widest feature range, or you work on Windows or Linux.

vs Shapr3D

Shapr3D is polished and works well on iPad with a pencil. It has a free tier with limits, and its full version is a yearly subscription (listed at about $299 a year for Pro).

SimplMaker keeps everything local and has no subscription. It has no iPad version.

Pick Shapr3D ifyou want to design on an iPad.

vs Onshape

Onshape is professional browser-based CAD. Its free plan makes every document public; keeping work private means a paid plan, which starts around $1,500 per user per year.

SimplMaker's files never leave your Mac.

Pick Onshape ifyou collaborate in real time, or you're fine working in the open.

vs Plasticity

Plasticity is the closest in business model: a one-time purchase (from about $150) that includes a year of updates, after which you keep the version you have. It is aimed at concept and surface modelling.

SimplMaker is aimed at dimensioned, parametric parts: brackets, enclosures, fixtures, things where a number changes and the part must follow.

Pick Plasticity ifyou model organic shapes or surfaces more than mechanical parts.

vs Tinkercad

Tinkercad is free, runs in the browser and is the easiest way to start. You build by combining shapes, so there's no feature timeline, no parameters and no constraints.

When you want to change a hole size at step 3 of 40 without redoing the rest, that's what SimplMaker adds.

Pick Tinkercad ifyou're starting out, or teaching someone who is.

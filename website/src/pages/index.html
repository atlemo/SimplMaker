01 · Model

Change a number.
The part updates.

Sketch a shape, give it sizes, and lift it into 3D.

Sketch

Lines, arcs, circles and splines, held in place by rules called constraints, like "parallel" or "equal".

Dimensions

Type a number or a formula. Change it later and the whole part follows.

Extrude

Drag the arrow or type a height. Add to what's there, cut away from it, or start a separate body.

Timeline

Every step is kept. Go back, change it, and watch the rest rebuild.

Make your first part

02 · Split

Too big?
Cut it down.

Cut a part into pieces that fit your printer, then glue, pin or magnet it back together.

Your printer

Pick it from the list or type your own build volume. The pieces are planned to fit it.

Smart cuts

The fewest pieces, cut evenly, turned any way up. Or place every cut yourself.

Connectors

Pins, keys, dovetails, dowels or magnets, so the pieces find each other again.

Tunable fit

Tight, standard, loose or your own number, because every printer is a little different.

03 · Mold · Experimental

Make it once.
Cast it often.

Wrap a printable mold round any body, and pour.

Auto mold

Pick a body. SimplMaker builds the block, splits it in two or four, and carves out the shape. You print the pieces.

Gate and vents

Where the silicone goes in and the air comes out. Placed for you, movable by you.

Keys

Little cones on the seam, so the halves only ever meet one way.

Release check

Undercuts glow red before the silicone finds them.

How molds work

04 · Render

Take its
picture.

Set the part on a floor, pick a light, and take the picture.

Floor

A floor that catches the shadow, so the part sits instead of floating.

Light

Move the light and the shadow follows. Soft by default, studio when you want it.

Materials

Plastic, metal, glass or wood, one click each.

Export

Save a picture, with or without the background.

About

Why it's free
and open source.

Makers share what they know, and SimplMaker stands on other people's open-source work, Open CASCADE above all. Passing it on seemed only fair.

This also means if something breaks, you can see why. If something is missing, you can add it. And if I ever get hit by a bus, the app doesn't go with me.

It is still a one-person project, so patience is appreciated and bug reports with a screenshot are adored. The license is the GNU GPL, version 3 or later; the license page has the details.

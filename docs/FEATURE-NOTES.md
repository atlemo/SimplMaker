# Feature notes

How the app behaves, feature by feature, written for people working on the code. What users read is the documentation at [simplmaker.com/docs](https://simplmaker.com/docs/), whose source is in `website/src/docs/`. When you change how something works, update both.

## Using it

SimplMaker is a normal Mac document app, with New, Open Recent, autosave, versions and parts as tabs of one window (see "Tabs are back" below). It opens on the **Start page** (a workspace of the window, see "Start and Render are workspaces too" below): create a new part, open the demo part, open a file, or pick one of the recent parts listed on the right (double-click or Return). There is no separate welcome window any more; **Window → Start** (⇧⌘1) goes back to the page. New to CAD? **Take the tutorial** on the Start page (or **Help → Tutorial: Make a Desk Organizer**) starts a guided tutorial of about ten minutes: a card at the bottom of the view walks you through making a desk organizer: five named parameters, a sketch and an extrude for the floor, then a second sketch on top of the floor that traces its outline, insets it as a guide, and hangs three sections from the guide with sizes typed as expressions and one Equal constraint, an extrude that raises the walls, and a last step that changes `width` and `depth` and watches the walls and every section follow. What to use next pulses in the toolbar, and the card follows what you actually do: it moves on when the part shows a step is done, steps back if you undo it, and says what is off (a rectangle drawn with plain numbers, a sketch on the wrong face, an inset that went outwards, walls left separate from the floor). The **‹** button goes back a step and undoes it. **Do it for me** does the current step the way the interface would; **Leave tutorial** (or ✕) closes the card, and **Help → Resume Tutorial** brings it back. The steps live in `Sources/SimplMaker/Tutorial/`. **File → Open Example Parts** (just under Open Recent) has three parts to learn from: a plate with a hole, a pin hole cut from a sketch on its top face, two filleted corners and a chamfered edge (⌥⌘N), an L-bracket braced by two ribs, each grown from a sketched line, and an advanced five-body assembly: a manifold block, an O-ring, a flange, a pipe swept along a curved path and four threaded bolts, each body in its own colour.

- **Parameters** (left panel, under the Browser): edit any expression, e.g. set `width` to `90 mm`, or `thickness` to `1 in`, or `hole_d` to `depth / 3`. Press Return and the model rebuilds.
- **Timeline** (the bar along the bottom of the window, toggled from the View menu): select a feature to edit it in the inspector on the right. Right-click a feature to suppress or delete it. A red triangle means the feature failed; hover over it to see why. The features after it still rebuild.
- **Sketch:**
  1. Click **Sketch** in the toolbar (or Sketch → New Sketch…). The three origin planes appear at the origin; click **XY**, **XZ** or **YZ**, or click a flat face of a body. Esc cancels. If a flat face is already selected, the sketch goes straight onto it. The view turns to face the sketch plane. (Sketch → New Sketch On still picks a plane directly.)
  2. Draw with the tools in the toolbar. Keys: **L** line (click point after point; Esc ends the chain), **B** spline (a smooth curve through the points you click; press and drag to pull a handle, click the first point to close the shape), **R** rectangle, **C** circle, **A** arc (center, start, end), **P** point, **D** distance, **I** dimension, **S** select.
     - **F** fillet: click a corner where two lines meet to round it.
     - **T** trim: click the part of a curve to cut away, up to where other curves cross it.
     - **E** extend: click near the end of a line or arc to extend it to the next curve.
     - **Offset:** select curves (lines, arcs, circles, and spline/Bézier runs), then press **Offset**. The offset shows live beside the originals with an arrow on it: drag the arrow to set the distance (drag it back through the curve to go to the other side), or type a number or expression in the field next to it (negative goes the other way). Return or **OK** keeps it as one undo step; Esc or **Cancel** takes it back. A positive distance goes outward on a closed shape, and to the left of an open chain's direction. Lines and arcs stay linked to their originals by a distance dimension; a Bézier's offset is a close fit made of several curves (no dimension), and is refused when the distance is more than the curve's tightest bend allows on the inside.
     - Clicking an existing point connects to it.
     - Clicking a curve puts the new point on that curve.
     - Nearly horizontal or vertical lines snap and get a constraint.
  3. **Dimensions:** with **I**, click a line (then empty space) for its length, a circle for its diameter, an arc for its radius, or two points, a point and a line, or two lines. Type a value or an expression such as `width / 2` in the popover that appears. Click a dimension label to change it later.
     - **Distance:** with **D**, click two items and type the distance between them, for example to put a hole 4 mm from the edge of a rectangle. A point, a line or a circle's center point is measured as usual; a circle or arc is measured from its *edge* (to measure from the middle, click its center point instead). Two points can be measured straight, or only horizontally or vertically, with the buttons in the popover. Two lines must be parallel. The circle moves to keep the distance and keeps its size, and the edge it is measured from stays where it is (likewise the point or line you click second). The distance is drawn with extension lines and arrows; with the Select tool, drag its line to move it out of the way.
  4. **Constraints:** select entities with **S** (⇧-click to add more), then pick from the **Constrain** menu or the inspector: coincident, horizontal, vertical, parallel, perpendicular, tangent, equal, midpoint, concentric, fix. Small symbols next to the geometry show each constraint (**H**, **V**, **∥**, **⊥**, **T**, **=**…). Click one to select it and press ⌫ to delete it. You can hide them under View → Show Constraints.
  5. With the select tool, drag points, lines or circle rims. The geometry follows as far as the constraints allow. ⌫ deletes the selection.
  6. The status bar shows the degrees of freedom left. Curves turn black when the sketch is fully constrained. Press **Return** or **Finish Sketch** when done.
- **Extrude:** select a sketch in the timeline. Its closed regions (profiles) show shaded; click one or more (⇧-click), then press **Extrude**. Drag the orange arrow to set the distance; the model updates as you drag. Or type the distance in the inspector, where you also set the operation (new body, join, cut, intersect) and direction.
  - **Extrude types:** the small arrow beside the Extrude button picks how far it reaches: **One Direction** (a distance to one side of the sketch), **Two Directions** (a distance to each side, with an arrow and a typed field for each; Tab moves between them) or **Mirrored** (one length shared evenly, half to each side). The button then works that way until you pick another, and the inspector's Extent picker changes it afterwards (it also has Through all and To face).
- **Edit a sketch later:** right-click it in the timeline → **Edit Sketch**, or use the inspector. The model is rolled back to that point while you edit.
- **Fillet / Chamfer:**
  1. Click edges in the viewport (⇧-click to add more).
  2. Press **Fillet** or **Chamfer** in the toolbar.
  3. Set the radius or distance in the inspector. It can be an expression like `corner_r / 2`.
  - **Chamfer types:** the small arrow beside the Chamfer button picks how it is measured: **Equal Distance Chamfer**, **Distance and Angle Chamfer** (a distance on one face and the angle to it) or **Two Distance Chamfer** (a distance on each face). The button then works that way until you pick another. The last two show an arrow and a size field for each value (Tab moves between them), and a Flip button (or the inspector's switch) puts the first distance on the edge's other face. With faces picked, the first distance is on the picked face.
- **Loft along a path** (centerline loft): a Loft can take a **Path**, an open chain of sketch curves from the first profile's plane to the last's (`LoftFeature.path`, picked with the Loft tool after the profiles, or in the Inspector; `path_sketch` + `path` on the AI tool `create_loft`). The kernel (`sk_loft_along`) cuts the path at those two planes and sweeps the sections along it with OCCT's pipe shell; every profile stays where it was drawn. Where the path meets every profile within 3° of a right angle the sections stay square to the path and turn with it (constant binormal: the path sketch's normal). Otherwise the profiles must be parallel, and the sections stay parallel to them and only move sideways. The reason for the two modes: OCCT's turning sweep through sections that stand at a slant to the path thins out and then collapses to zero volume while still reporting a valid solid, so a slanted path with non-parallel profiles is an error, and a result with next to no volume for its size is refused. Holes in the profiles follow the same path. "Straight sides" does not apply. This is not a guide-rail loft (rails on the surface); that would need the side surfaces built from a curve network.
- **Other sketches while sketching:** the sketches shown in the browser are drawn behind the one being edited (`DocumentModel.backgroundSketches`, the inactive sketch colour, pick id 0 so clicks go through), and their points and curves, flattened onto the sketch plane, are added to `SketchReferences` without a model edge, so the cursor snaps to them by position only (no projection, no constraint). A curve seen edge-on becomes one segment. Switch: **Show Other Sketches** in the sketch's Inspector (`showOtherSketches`, on by default, stored inverted as `hideOtherSketches`). **Project Edge** (U) also takes curves of other sketches: a `SketchProjection` whose `edge.body` is a sketch's id and `edge.edge` a curve's id (no new file field; a sketch makes no body, so the ids can't clash). `Regenerator.sketchCurveSnap` says how the curve lands on the plane (line; circle/arc on a parallel plane as itself; circle/arc/ellipse edge-on as the straight stretch it covers; Bézier exactly, and a slanted circle as Bézier pieces), and `followProjections` refreshes it on every rebuild as the kind it was made as. Only sketches earlier in the timeline are offered (their curves become clickable while the tool is on). A sketch's `references` do not list what it projects (true for body edges before this too), so reordering the source after the sketch gives a rebuild error rather than being prevented.
- **Intersect and Project Again** (sketch References; keys Q and W): **Intersect** brings in where the sketch plane cuts a body, as projections. The kernel call `sk_body_section` (`KBody.section(planeThrough:normal:)`) cuts the body with the plane (`BRepAlgoAPI_Section`) and returns an edge-only body whose edges are named after the face of the body each lies on ("#2", "#3" for a face cut in several places). Each curve becomes an ordinary `SketchProjection` whose `edge` is `EdgeRef(body: <body id>, edge: "section:<name>")` (`Regenerator.sectionPrefix`), so cleanup, stale detection and the file format needed nothing new; `Regenerator.followProjection` recognises the prefix, cuts the body again (once per body per rebuild, `SectionCache`) and re-places the points as for a body edge (`sectionSnaps` lists the pieces: straight, round when it faces the sketch, any other curve as Bézier pieces). Chosen over a one-off snapshot because every other reference in a sketch follows the model, and a cut that doesn't would be the odd one out. The cost is a boolean per body per rebuild, and a piece that changes face (or splits differently) goes stale rather than jumping. **Project Again** (`Sketch.reattachProjection`, tool `.projectAgain`) replaces the `edge` of a projection of the same kind (straight, arc, circle, curve) and re-places its points; the points, curves and what is tied to them stay. The session takes the selected projection (or the only stale one, or a click on its curve), then a click on the new body edge or sketch curve. Because a sketch with a stale projection fails to build, `editSketch` opens it on its plane anyway and the rebuild still finds the stale set (`DocumentModel`, the `followModel` call), which Clean Up Stale Projections also needed. A straight projection takes the direction of the new edge.
- **Twist Sweep** (⇧W): pushes sketch profiles straight along the sketch's normal for a **Length** while turning them about that line, from a **Start angle** to an **End angle**. The **Curve** (default 1) spreads the turn: at the fraction *t* of the way it is start + (end − start) × *t*^curve. **Slide across / up** moves the profile sideways in the sketch's x and y over the length (a lean, or a blade's rise), and the profile turns about its own middle unless a turning point is set. It is `TwistSweepFeature` in CADCore, built by `sk_twist_sweep` in the kernel: a smooth loft (`BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections`) through a copy of the profile about every 3° of turn (8 to 80 sections), which `sk_loft` and the twist share through `loftFaces`. Join, cut and intersect work as for a sweep, so a twisted plate intersected with an extruded outline is a fan blade. The angle always turns about the sketch's normal, so a flipped sweep keeps its sign. No drag handle yet.
- **Navigation:**
  - Trackpad: two-finger scroll pans, ⇧ + scroll orbits, pinch zooms, rotate spins the view (in a sketch it turns the sketch on its paper).
  - Mouse: drag on empty space orbits, right- or middle-drag pans, the wheel zooms. In a sketch, ⌥-drag turns the sketch on its paper (the pointer takes the picture round with it; add ⇧ to land on every 15°), the two curved arrows by the view cube turn it a quarter at a time, and the cube's faces and the house turn it upright again.
  - **Settings → Navigation** (⌘,) changes all of it: **Navigate like** copies the mouse buttons and keys of Blender, Fusion, Onshape or SolidWorks; **Customize buttons and keys** sets what each mouse button, the trackpad's scroll and the wheel do with each of ⇧, ⌃ and ⌥ held; and there are switches to invert orbit and pan (up/down and left/right separately) and zoom, plus orbit and zoom speed.
  - The **View** menu has Fit All (⌘0), Top/Front/Right/Home views (⌘1–⌘4), Orthographic Projection (⌥⌘O) and Show/Hide Inspector (⌥⌘I). Esc clears the selection.
- **Keys:** the Create, Modify and Inspect menus show a single letter for each tool: **E** Extrude, **R** Revolve, **W** Sweep (⇧**W** Twist Sweep), **L** Loft, **B** Rib, **P** Pipe, **X** Box, **Y** Cylinder, **G** Sphere, **U** Torus, **K** Coil, **N** Construction Plane, **V** Canvas, **F** Fillet, **C** Chamfer, **H** Hole, **D** Thread, **S** Shell, **O** Project, **M** Move (and rotate), **Z** Scale, **J** Combine, **Q** Mirror, **T** Pattern, **I** Measure, and **A** for Appearance. While you sketch, the letters pick sketch tools instead, and in a text field they type as usual.
- **Body colours:** press **A** (or **View → Show Appearance**) to open the Appearance panel. Select bodies in the view (a face or edge counts for its body) or in the panel's body list (⇧-click to add more), then click a colour, or choose any colour with **Custom Colour**. **Default** puts the standard colour back. Right-click a body → **Appearance…** opens the panel for it. Colours are saved in the file, can be undone, and need no rebuild.
- **Light and dark:** the viewport follows light and dark mode, and draws thicker lines when Increase Contrast is on.
- **Files:** documents are `.simplmaker` files (JSON). They store only parameters and features; geometry is always rebuilt. **File → Export…** writes the bodies to STL or STEP, as one part or one file per body.
- **Threads:** **Modify → Thread** (D), or right-click a round face → **Thread…**, then click the side of a shaft or the wall of a hole. It cuts a modeled thread, external on a shaft and internal in a hole, in the size that suits the face: an 8 mm shaft gets M8, a 6.8 mm hole (the M8 tap drill) gets an M8 thread. The inspector picks the standard (ISO Metric, ISO Metric Fine, UNC or UNF) and size, and shows the pitch, diameters and tap drill. The thread runs the full length of the face or a set distance (drag the arrow), from the free end (a shaft's tip, a hole's mouth) or the other end, right- or left-handed. The face is turned or bored to the thread's diameters first, and the thread uses the basic 60° profile, without tolerance classes.
- **Project:** **Modify → Project** (O) throws sketch profiles onto a body like a projector, so they follow its surface, flat or curved: text on a cylinder, a logo on a sphere. Click the profiles (the letters of a text, say), then click the face to project onto, or press Return to use the whole body when it is the only one. The profiles land along the sketch's normal (or against it: **Flip direction** in the inspector, set to point at the body when you start) on the faces that face the sketch, so a round post gets them on its sketch side only. **Emboss** raises them by the height and joins them to the body; **Deboss** cuts them in by the depth. Drag the arrow on the surface or type the height or depth (an expression works). The inspector can limit the projection to picked faces and pick the profiles again. It is a projection, not a wrap: the shapes are not unrolled, so they stretch where the surface turns away from the sketch, and nothing lands on a face seen edge-on.
- **Import STEP and STL:** **File → Import → STEP…** (⇧⌘I) or **STL…** adds a `.step`/`.stp` or `.stl` file to the timeline as an **Import** feature: one body with all the file's solids, named after the file, in millimetres whatever unit the file uses. The file's contents are stored in the part, so it rebuilds without the original. Fillet, cut, shell, move or rotate it like any other body. **Replace File…** in the inspector swaps in a new version (STEP or STL); features after the import keep their edges and faces as long as the new file lists its faces in the same order. An STL mesh must be watertight (closed, no gaps) and is read in millimetres; its triangles are sewn into a solid and the ones lying in one plane are merged back into single faces, so a box is six faces, not twelve triangles. Curved shapes stay faceted, one flat face per triangle, and meshes of more than 60,000 triangles are refused.
- **Canvas (reference image):** **Create → Canvas (Reference Image)…** (**V**), or **Insert Image…** in the right-click menu of a face or construction plane. Select a flat face or plane first (or click one while the tool waits), then choose a PNG, JPEG, HEIC or TIFF file. The picture lies on that plane at its middle, upright as the view shows the plane, to trace over and measure against. The Inspector sets its **opacity** (a slider; it rebuilds nothing), width (the height follows the picture's proportions), position and turn, all as expressions, and can swap in another file. Clicks and sketching pass through it. The file is kept inside the part, and the browser lists it under Canvases, where the eye hides it.
- **Import SVG:** **File → Import → SVG as Sketch…** turns an SVG file into a sketch on the selected flat face or plane (the XY plane when nothing is selected), or adds it to the sketch you are editing. Paths (lines, arcs, cubic and quadratic curves), rectangles, circles, ellipses, polygons and polylines are read, with groups, `transform`s and `<use>` applied, and sized in millimetres from the file's `width`/`height` and `viewBox` (96 px to the inch when it has no size). The drawing's middle goes on the sketch origin. Circles and circular arcs stay exact; closed shapes become profiles you can extrude or cut. Text and images are left out, so convert text to outlines first. The result is plain, unconstrained geometry that you can dimension and edit like anything you drew yourself.
- **Move manipulator:** **Move** (M) puts one manipulator, for both moving and rotating, on the bodies or sketches you picked: three arrows slide along X, Y or Z, the three squares slide in a plane, the dot in the middle slides across the screen, and the three rings turn about X, Y or Z (drag a ring's dot). The part you grabbed last is lit, and its value is in the field beside it: type a distance or an angle there, or switch between Move and Rotate and X, Y, Z in the panel below. Distances and angles snap to round steps while you drag. It is one **Move** feature with X, Y, Z and Rotate X, Y, Z values, and the turns happen about the middle of what moves, X then Y then Z, before the move; a value that comes from a parameter asks before a drag replaces it with a number. Rotate features from older files still open, with their single arrow.
- **Scale:** select bodies (click a face, or pick them in the browser) or a sketch (in the browser, or click one of its profiles or curves), then **Scale** in the Modify group (or **Z**, or right-click → Scale Body / Scale Sketch). It adds a **Scale** feature with a factor you can type, or drag with the arrow. Bodies grow evenly about the middle of their bounding box (several together about the middle of them all) and keep their faces and edges, so later features still hold; handy for an imported STL or STEP file at the wrong size. A sketch scales about its own origin, including circles and text, and features built from the sketch after it use the scaled version. The sketch itself stays as drawn, so its dimensions still work, and the factor can be a parameter (`width / 40`).
- **Move and Scale inside a sketch:** the sketch toolbar's **Transform** group (also in the **Sketch** menu) works on the curves and points you have selected, or on the whole sketch when nothing is selected. **Move** puts a manipulator in the sketch plane on the selection: drag an arrow to slide along the sketch's x or y, the square or the dot to slide in both, or the ring to turn about the middle of the selection. The sketch follows live; let go to keep it (one undo step), or type an X, Y and angle in the fields under the manipulator and press Return. Pick another selection and it moves to it; Esc or the tool's button again turns it off. **Scale** still opens a box for a factor (about the middle of the selection, or the sketch origin). Circle radii, text height and the dimensions that measure what grows go along when scaling, and a quarter turn swaps horizontal and vertical constraints. A constraint or dimension that would pull the geometry back (a fixed point, or a distance from the origin) puts it back with a message. Text moves, scales and turns (its baseline turns about its anchor, and the angle is saved with it). A new or reopened sketch starts with the Select tool. The **Scale** tool in the Modify group instead adds a Scale feature to the timeline.
- **Pattern inside a sketch:** **Pattern** in the sketch toolbar's Transform group copies the selected curves (or the whole sketch, with nothing selected) in a **Rectangular** grid or a **Circular** run. The copies show live and stay **linked**: they are rebuilt from the originals, so moving, resizing or dimensioning an original, or changing a parameter, moves every copy. For a grid, drag the arrow along x or along y to set the spacing between columns or rows, and type the counts and spacings in the fields (the original counts as one; a negative spacing goes the other way). For a circle, drag the ring to set how far the copies spread (360° goes all the way round, less puts the last copy at the end of the angle), type the count, and click a sketch point to copy around it (the sketch origin to begin with). Counts, spacings and the spread can be expressions or parameters (`n`, `width / 2`); dragging replaces the expression with a number. Return or **OK** keeps the pattern as one undo step; Esc or **Cancel** takes it back. To change a pattern later, click one of its copies with the Select tool; **Delete** (or ⌫) in the panel removes the copies and leaves the originals. The copies are read-only: you can't draw to them, constrain them or drag them, but you can extrude their profiles like any other. A turned copy of text turns with it. A pattern makes at most 400 copies, and curves added to the sketch later are not part of it.

## The top of the window

The window's own toolbar (which keeps the traffic lights and the tabs) holds the pills: the browser toggle, undo and redo, the workspace selector, and Hide UI and Inspector at the right. The tools are **islands** under it (`IslandBar`, `Sources/SimplMaker/IslandToolbar.swift`): each tool group is a glass plate of its own (`.island()`), floating over the view like the panels, with the panels starting below the row (`DocumentPanels.topChrome`, `ViewportView.chromeTop` for the camera and the view cube). The tools used to sit in the toolbar's middle, with a titlebar strip on macOS 15; both are gone. The row is measured on a copy that is never shown (`measureToolRows`) and narrows by taking pinned tools out of each group, then becomes one menu, as before. In Sketch mode the islands draw in the contrast ink on a tinted band (`ToolbarFade`, no material), and the pills in the toolbar do the same. Hiding the system toolbar altogether also hides the traffic lights and the tab bar, which is why the pills stay in it.

**Workspaces** (`Workspace.swift`): `DocumentModel.workspace` is Start, Design, Split, Mold or Render, a way of working with the same document, not a feature (not saved, no undo step). All five are always offered (`Workspace.available`); Mold and Split used to be hidden behind a defaults flag while they were built. Switching finishes a sketch being edited and drops a tool waiting for picks. The undo and redo buttons refresh on the undo manager's group-closed, undo and redo notifications, **not** the checkpoint one: reading `canUndo` posts it, which loops.

**Start and Render are workspaces too.** Start is `StartPage.swift`, a page as big as the window laid over the view and the panels (the toolbar stays over it, so the selector is always there; the covered content is hidden from VoiceOver); it is modelled on the iPad's `PadHome` and is the only place the recent parts list is drawn (`RecentPartsList`, in `RecentParts.swift`; the old welcome window is gone). An empty part opens on it (`DocumentModel.openingWorkspace`) and the window moves on to Design when the part gets features or the tutorial starts (`ContentView`'s onChange). The old small "Start your part" card is only for the iPad now (`DocumentPanels.showsStartCard`). Render is the existing Render view: `setWorkspace(.render)` calls `setRenderView(true)`, and `setRenderView` moves the selector, so the View menu and the context menu stay in step; the tool row is hidden there (`Workspace.hasTools`), and running a design tool or starting a sketch (`ensureDesignWorkspace`, Mac only) returns to Design and turns the view off. Pressing Render again reopens its options panel.

**Tabs in the toolbar** (SUPERSEDED, see "Tabs are back" at the end of this section; kept for the lessons on the system's tab bar): parts open as tabs of one window (`tabbingMode = .preferred`), but the system's tab bar is a row of its own, so it is hidden and the toolbar's middle item draws the tabs (`WindowTabStrip`, a capsule beside the selector). The tabs are still the system's (`NSWindowTabGroup`), so Window → Move Tab to New Window and Merge All Windows work; dragging a tab out is gone. The bar is hidden by hiding its **titlebar accessory** (`SystemTabBar`), not with `toggleTabBar`: that is one setting for all windows, it does nothing for a window with several tabs, and every window toggling at once made them flip it back and forth (100 % CPU, preferences rewritten each time). The strip is only there while the window has more than one tab (with one part the middle item is an empty 1 pt item, which keeps the trailing pill at the far right; the part's name is then shown nowhere). Pills in the toolbar are separate items each followed by a 6 pt gap (`DS.Spacing.space3`), the trailing pill has 6 pt to the window's edge, and the island row is spaced 6 pt too; the panels keep `Panel.inset` (8 pt). The strip is as wide as the toolbar has room for (`WindowTabStrip.reserved`, a measured guess that has to leave some slack: 580 pushed the trailing pill into the toolbar's » overflow menu in a 1374 pt window, 625 fits; the toolbar sizes a middle item to its content). The + button makes a document the way File → New does and joins it to the group by hand if the system did not.

**Launch without a window to restore.** A document app that starts with nothing to restore (or has its Dock icon clicked with no window open) shows the system's **Open panel with a New Document button** instead of an empty part: macOS's `NSShowAppCentricOpenPanelInsteadOfUntitledFile`, on by default. The welcome window used to cover this; once it was gone the panel showed up "sometimes" (reproduced with a fresh bundle id). `SimplMakerApp.init` registers the default as NO and `AppDelegate` answers yes to `applicationShouldOpenUntitledFile` and `applicationShouldHandleReopen`, so an empty part opens, on the Start page. The Dock-icon case itself is untested (it needs an unlocked screen); the launch case was checked.

**Tabs keep their size.** A tab's close button always has its room (opacity 0, no hit testing, unless the pointer is on the tab or it is the selected one), so hovering does not change the width and the tabs next to it do not jump.

**The ribbon replaced the islands** (`Ribbon.swift`). The tool groups used to be one glass island each, plus a pill for every group of buttons in the title bar: too many bubbles. Now the title bar has two pills (`ToolbarLeadingPill`: browser, undo, redo; `ToolbarTrailingPills`: Hide UI, inspector) and the tools are one panel, `RibbonBar`: the big `WorkspaceButton`, a `RibbonDivider`, then the tool groups with dividers between them (no wrapper per group; `DesignToolbar` and `SketchToolbar` just put dividers in their `HStack`). The ribbon is an overlay of the window's content **above the Start page**, so the workspace button is there on Start and in Render too (the tool row is only drawn where `Workspace.hasTools`). The room the tool row has is the window's width less `RibbonBar.chrome`. The workspace list (`WorkspaceList`) is a glass panel the window draws under the button, not an NSPopover: a popover under a button at the window's left edge stuck out past the window. A click elsewhere or Esc closes it. The glyph, colour, summary and contents of each workspace are in `Workspace`'s Mac extension in `Ribbon.swift`. No ribbon tab row (Model, Surface…): the groups are flat.

**Parts opened from a button or menu come as tabs** (`PartWindows` in `WindowTabs.swift`). Only the system's own File → New joins the front window's tab group; SwiftUI's `newDocument` and `openDocument` (the Start page's New Part, the demo and the tutorial, Open Example Parts, a recent part, Help → Tutorial) opened a window of their own each time ("New Part just keeps opening new windows"). `PartWindows.open { … }` runs the opener, finds the window that appears (titled, not there before) and adds it to the tab group of the front part window with `addTabbedWindow`. The system's Open… panel is not wrapped.

**Start has no ribbon; made-on-purpose parts open in Design.** The ribbon is drawn in every workspace but Start (`showsRibbon`), so the Start page needs no room for it and its way out is on the page (Sketch, Box, Cylinder, New Part, the "Go on modelling" row, and Window → Start to come back). A part opens on Start only when it is the first of a launch: `DocumentGroup(newDocument:)` makes `CADFile(startsInDesign: PartWindows.hasOpenPart)`, so ⌘N or a new tab with another part open starts in Design, and so does the Start page's New Part (`startsInDesign: true`). The Start block's drop shadow is the iPad's: a black copy of the shape behind it, blurred, with the shape cut out of the middle so the block stays clear. `PartWindows` looks for the anchor window among the key, main and ordered windows, since a background app has no key window.

**The ribbon is the window's top bar** (SUPERSEDED by the system title bar and the floating capsule, see "System title bar" below; the traffic-light code described in this and the next two paragraphs is gone): `WindowSizeMemory` makes the titlebar transparent (`titlebarAppearsTransparent`, `.fullSizeContentView`) and `ContentView` hides the toolbar background and title, so the window keeps only the traffic lights; the ribbon overlay (`ignoresSafeArea` at the top) is as wide as the window, with `RibbonBar.windowControls` (72 pt) of room at its left for the lights, then the workspace button, the tools and `UndoRedoButtons` at the far right, over a `.bar` background with a hairline under it. The empty parts of the ribbon drag the window and a double click zooms it (`WindowDragArea`). The browser, Hide UI and inspector buttons are gone (View menu and shortcuts only). With more than one tab, `WindowTabStrip` is a row under the ribbon; on Start there is neither ribbon nor tab row. Layout: `barHeight` is measured from the very top of the window; the panels start below it (`chromeTop` = `barHeight` less the safe area's top), and the viewport takes `ribbonBottom` (absolute). Mind that `PartWindows`' "is a part open" answer for the new-document closure is `OpenParts`, a lock-protected counter: that closure runs on an operation queue, and asking AppKit for windows there crashes the launch (it did, in 138a40c). ⌘N and File → New go through `PartWindows` too (the menu item is replaced), so they join the front window's tabs.

**A Start page is not a tab.** A part that is empty and on the Start workspace is a launcher: `DocumentWindow` tells its `WindowTabs` (`setStartPage`), which records the window in `StartPageWindows`, and every tab strip leaves such windows out (so the strip needs two real parts to show). New Part on the Start page, and ⌘N / File → New while the front part is one, turn that part into Design in place instead of opening another window; the demo, a recent part or an example opened from it close it once the new window has joined the tabs (`PartWindows.join(closing:)`). **Traffic lights:** the ribbon is taller than a titlebar, so `WindowControls.place` makes the titlebar view twice the axis tall and centres the three buttons in it. The axis is the middle of the ribbon, which is the line through undo and redo: the ribbon reports its height (`WindowTabs.setRibbonHeight`, from `DocumentWindow`), `RibbonMetrics.defaultAxis` (37) is used until then, and with no ribbon (Start, or the UI hidden) they go back to a titlebar's place (`titlebarAxis`, 14). AppKit moves them whenever the window's appearance changes (Sketch mode flips it for the tint), on resizes, activation and leaving full screen, so `WindowControls.watch` re-places them on the frame-change notifications of the three buttons and their containers. A debug run with SIMPLMAKER_FORCE_TINT=1 shows the tinted sketch window without it being the active one. No hairline under the ribbon; the tab row has `Panel.inset` above and below it.

**Traffic lights 7 pt further in, one top bar.** `WindowControls.inset` (7 pt) moves the three buttons in from AppKit's own x, as Finder and other toolbar windows have them (AppKit's x is noted the first time, `standardX`, and kept, so later placements do not add up); the ribbon leaves `windowControls` = 72 + that much at its left. The ribbon and the tab row are one bar: the `.bar` background is on the whole top bar (in Sketch mode the tinted band is behind it), and the tab strip has `Panel.inset` (8 pt) on every side, so in both modes the strip has the same room above and below it and the panels start `Panel.inset` under the bar. A window that starts without a ribbon (the Start page) sets its lights to a titlebar's place at once (`onChange(of: showsRibbon, initial: true)`). In a forced-tint debug run (SIMPLMAKER_FORCE_TINT=1) in a window that is not active the first sketch tool's icon is invisible (it draws in the accent colour, since `toolsOnTint` follows the active state): not a bug of the real, active window.

**Dragging the window by the ribbon** (`WindowDragArea`): the view behind the ribbon returns `mouseDownCanMoveWindow = true` and lets AppKit do the drag; a double-click gesture zooms. Calling `performDrag(with:)` from `mouseDown` as well (the first version) made every drag log "Window move completed without beginning", because AppKit started the drag too. A synthetic drag in a test copy does not move the window but does show the warning, so count the warnings in the log (0 now) rather than watching the window.

**System title bar, floating ribbon capsule (2026-11-19, `0fde0c5`).** The window has the standard title bar again (traffic lights and the part's name placed by the system); `WindowSizeMemory` no longer makes it transparent and the code that moved the traffic lights by hand (`WindowControls`) is deleted. The ribbon is one rounded capsule floating under it (`RibbonBar`), tinted in Sketch mode; the panels start below it (`chromeTop`). The workspace button, the tool groups and Undo/Redo are inside the capsule; Start has none.

**Panel material and style.** Panels draw on `panelMaterial(in:)` (`PanelMaterialBackground`, `DesignSystem.swift`): `Preferences.panelStyle` is Liquid Glass (default, macOS 26 and later; older systems fall back to the blur) or the system blur, and `Preferences.panelMaterial` (thin, regular, thick) is how frosted: on glass a frosted layer behind it (none for thin), on the blur the material plus white in light mode. Settings > General has both pickers (the style only where glass exists); Sketch background moved there from Viewport. Tests that measure ink in a drawn panel (`GridLayoutTests.inkRuns`) count only dark pixels, because the panel now has a pale fill of its own.

**Tabs are back (2026-11-20).** `PartWindows.swift`: a native `NSWindowTabGroup`, with a flat strip as a title-bar accessory (`WindowTabStrip`, 34 pt, hidden with one part), each tab with the part's picture as a small icon, background tabs with a faint fill. Every way of opening a part (File > New and Open, the Start page, Finder and Dock opens) joins the front window's tabs. A Start page is still not a tab. `OpenParts` is a lock-protected counter because the new-document closure runs off the main thread.

**Start page, recent parts.** The page has New Part, Open…, the tutorial, Sketch, the demo part and a button to the YouTube tutorials (Box and Cylinder are gone). The recent list holds 20 parts as picture cards; `RecentPictures` draws a new picture each time a part is opened or first saved and keeps it in the caches folder (the picture in the file is only as new as its last save).

**Render workspace tool row** (`RenderToolbar.swift`): the quality segments, the turntable, a button that shows and hides the options panel, and Export. The options panel keeps the floor, room, light, shadows and the Advanced tab.

## The Mold workspace (Phase 1; Phase 2 and four pieces below)

A rigid, printable two-part mold around one body, to cast silicone or resin. On for everyone since 1.2.9 (the `moldWorkspace` flag is gone). The plan and the Phase 0 kernel results are in `docs/MOLD-PLAN.md`.

**A mold is not a feature.** `CADDocument.molds` (`[MoldSetup]`, `Sources/CADCore/Mold.swift`) is stored beside the features, optional like folders (old files are untouched, an empty list writes no key, every setting is optional when reading so later versions can add one). `ignoringPresentation` clears it, so changing a mold rebuilds no feature. Sizes (wall, gate, vents, keys, offset) are expressions like every other size. Undo goes through `mutate`.

**`MoldBuilder`** (`Sources/CADKernel/MoldBuilder.swift`, pure kernel, tested in `MoldBuilderTests`): block around the body (bounds + wall) → gate and vents fused into one tool and cut from the plain block → split at the parting plane → cone keys on the plain halves (pegs on the front half, sockets with clearance in the back one, each set fused first) → the body subtracted from each half last. About twice as fast as cutting the body out first on a heavy mesh. It refuses a body that is not `isValid`, a plane that misses the body or leans (only planes square to X, Y or Z), and retries a split with the plane nudged by 0.05 / -0.05 / 0.2 mm when the halves come out invalid (a seam of a round face). Keys shrink to fit a thin wall (warning); a key that would sit in a channel is left out. The gate goes in at the highest point along "up" (the middle of a flat top), snapped onto a vertical parting plane so the split makes two half-channels. `cavityVolume` (the silicone) is exact: block − halves + pegs − sockets. `printLayout` turns each half parting face up, on the floor, side by side.

**The kernel can drop a cavity without an error** (found by the user with a sphere, 2026-11-19: only one half had the pocket). `block − body` came back valid, with no cavity, when the gate's axis ran exactly through the sphere's pole and the sphere was at (13.7, -8.2); the same sphere at the origin was fine. So the builder measures what the body's subtraction took out (`minimumRemoved`, against the body's volume less what the channels already took) and tries again in the other order and with the plane nudged before it gives up with an error, and the automatic gate sits 0.0137 / 0.0071 mm off the exact highest point (not on the sphere's own axis). `MoldRoundBodySweepTests` builds 72 spheres, cylinders and tori at random places across all three axes. Any new boolean in the builder deserves the same kind of volume check.

**`MoldStudio.swift`** is the model side: `rebuildMolds()` runs the builder on a background thread (`KernelActivity` begin/end, a generation counter so a newer request wins) after each rebuild of the part (`apply` → `moldBodyChanged`) and after each mold change (`replace` → `moldSettingsChanged`), but only while the Mold workspace is showing (otherwise `moldsStale` and it builds on entering). The viewport's scene in the workspace is `visibleMeshes` → `moldShownMeshes`: the body (ghosted by `displayOpacity`), then the two halves (tinted by `displayColor`). The workspace hides the timeline and brings it back. Picking is off in the workspace; `moldPicking` (vent, gate) makes a click on the body place a point (`focusPoint`), and shows only the body while it waits. "Open" is `moldOpenOffsets`, plugged into `explodeOffsets`.

**Screens** (`MoldViews.swift`, Mac only): ribbon groups Parting (X Y Z) · Gate & Vents · Keys · View · Export; left panels Molds and Bodies; right panel Mold (name, result: silicone mL, block size, warnings; parting plane, offset, pour direction, wall; gate; vents; keys; Open slider). Entry: the workspace button, a face's context menu "Make a Mold…", ⌘K "Make a Mold…". Export asks the usual format question and writes the halves laid out for printing.

**AI:** `create_mold` (make, change, delete), `get_mold`, `export_mold` and the design-guide topic `make_a_mold`, offered only while the workspace is switched on (`CADService+Mold.swift`).

**Choosing the body.** Entering Mold (or Split) with no mold yet draws every body, lit whole on hover; a click makes the mold or split of that body and hides the rest (`choosingWorkspaceBody`). The pointer button in the Bodies panel header brings the choice back, and deleting the last mold or split does too. The grid starts hidden in Mold; Show Grid flips a Mold-only setting while the workspace is on. A hand-placed gate stays at the height clicked, as vents do.

**Not done / known (Phase 1; most of it built later, see Phase 2):** vent and gate markers in the viewport (a placed vent only shows as a row in the panel and as a hole once the halves are back); undercut and pull-direction analysis (Phase 2); several bodies per mold; leaning planes; tongue-and-groove, clamps, shrinkage (Phase 3); no part-first order for sketch-based keys. The iPad project does not build for reasons that predate Mold (`RibbonDivider` and `PanelTouch.panelTrailing` in shared files); the Mold files add nothing to that list (checked with `xcodegen` + `xcodebuild`, 2026-11-19).

### Mold Phase 2: guidance (2026-11-19)

**Release analysis** (`MoldAnalysis`, `Sources/CADKernel/MoldAnalysis.swift`, pure, on the body's mesh): each triangle sits in the half in front of or behind the parting plane, and that half is pulled along (or against) the plane's normal; the triangle's surface normal (the mesh's vertex normals, exact on curved faces) against that direction says whether the half lets go (`free`), the face is parallel to the pull (`noDraft`, under 1° of lean) or hangs back against it (`undercut`, more than 1° the wrong way). A face is judged by its share (undercut when over 1% of its area, no draft when over 25%), so a curved face that merely meets the plane is not an undercut. Not found: shadowing (a face that faces the right way but sits behind another one along the pull), which needs rays; the note is in the guide.

**Casting** (`MoldSetup.casting`, silicone by default, not written when default): silicone only warns about an undercut (it is peeled out of a mild one); resin refuses the mold, before any boolean, but keeps the analysis, so the Check view still shows where. Resin also warns when over 25% of the surface has no draft. A new mold (and `create_mold`) starts on the axis with the least undercut, the shortest side among equals (`MoldAnalysis.bestAxis`).

**Vents** (`MoldVents.suggest`): the peaks of the body along "up", with their prominence found by walking down from the top and merging the regions that meet (union-find on the welded mesh); a peak counts at 3% of the height (at least 1 mm) above its saddle, peaks within 5 mm of a higher one are one, and the one the gate sits on is left out. A flat top gets its vent in the middle. Peaks only: the ceiling under an overhang traps air too and needs a hand-placed vent. "Auto Vents" in the ribbon and `auto_vents: true` in `create_mold` add them (never twice at the same place).

**Screens:** ribbon group Check (Release): the body drawn alone, each face coloured green / amber / red through the faceLooks hook (`moldCheckLooks`); the Check section of the inspector (undercut faces and share, no-draft share, legend); the Casting picker; markers at the gate (red) and each vent (purple), small balls added to the workspace's scene (`mold~<id>~gate`, `~vent<i>`), visible while the halves are hidden (Check view, picking a spot) or opened.

**Tests:** `MoldAnalysisTests` (box, disc-and-stem, sphere on all axes, casting rules, vents), more in `MoldStudioTests` and `AIMoldTests`. Test models wait for the build thread to let go before a test ends (a build still unwinding when the process exits segfaults it).

**The tool row is centred in the window** (2026-11-19, Design, Sketch and Mold alike): `RibbonBar` puts the window controls, the workspace button and undo/redo in an HStack and the tools over it in a ZStack, so they sit in the middle of the window whatever is at the sides; the divider that stood between the workspace button and the tools is gone. The room the layout may use is the window's width less `RibbonBar.sideRoom` at both sides (the left cluster is the wider one), so a row that no longer fits centred loses tools from its groups as before. Checked at 1080 pt: the tool row's middle is within 1 pt of the window's.

**Four-piece molds** (2026-11-19): `MoldSetup.pieces` (2 or 4, written only when 4) and `cross` (the axis of the second plane, nil = automatic). The second plane is square to X, Y or Z through the middle of the body; automatic picks a plane that holds the pour direction (the gate and vents are cut lengthways), and when parted across the pour direction the one with the least undercut, then the longer side. `MoldBuilder` splits the block at the parting plane, then each half at the second plane (`split(_:at:andAt:)`; the order of `MoldSetup.pieceIDs`: behind the parting plane first, each behind the second plane first; ids end `~behind~a`, `~behind~b`, `~front~a`, `~front~b`), puts the keys on both planes (`keys(on:at:shift:tag:)`: each piece gets only the keys in its own part of a plane; pegs on the piece in front of a plane, sockets behind), then cuts the body out of all four. `MoldResult.pieces` (`MoldPiece`: id, name, body, side, crossSide) replaces behind/front (kept as computed properties for two halves). `MoldAnalysis.analyse(... cross:)`: a piece is pulled in any direction between its two outward pulls, so a face's lean is the length of its normal's projection on those two when it looks towards both, else the better of the two: a groove that is an undercut for two halves is free for four (the face goes amber, parallel to the pull). The undercut messages say "four pieces" as a way out. UI: Pieces 2/4 in the ribbon, Pieces and Second cut in the Parting section of the inspector, Open moves each piece along both normals, four tints, Export lays out four pieces. MCP: `pieces`, `cross` on create_mold; get_mold gives pieces, cross, cross_point, cross_normal and four ids. Tests: `MoldFourPieceTests` (kernel, 8), plus cases in `MoldStudioTests`, `AIMoldTests`, `MoldSetupTests`. Checked in the real window (grooved block, `MoldSampleFile` writes it as `.groove`): 2 halves across Z show one undercut face, 4 pieces show none, Open draws four tinted pieces apart. Not checked there: the Export dialog with four pieces, dark mode, narrow window. Limits: pieces are pulled diagonally (a key on one plane stops a straight sideways slide until the pieces are first drawn apart), no shadowing, the second plane is always through the middle of the body (no offset), no leaning planes.

## The Split workspace: Split to Print (2026-11-19)

A workspace between Design and Mold: cut a body that is too big for the printer into pieces that fit it, with connectors. Not a timeline feature; follows the Mold pattern.

**Model** (`Sources/CADCore/PrintSplit.swift`): `PrintSplit` (stored as `CADDocument.printSplits`, optional key, tolerant decoding, cleared in `ignoringPresentation`), `PrinterPreset.catalog` (maker volumes, ids like `bambu-256`, `custom` + three expressions), `ConnectorKind` (none, pin, key, dovetail, dowel, magnet; `choose(_:)` brings its default size and length), `SplitFit` (tight 0.1, standard 0.2, loose 0.3, custom), `PrintSplitCut` (axis + position, `cuts == nil` means automatic by `counts`, 0 = automatic).

**Builder** (`Sources/CADKernel/PrintSplitBuilder.swift`): usable volume = printer − 2·margin → counts (`automaticCounts`: with turning tries the six axis assignments, fewest pieces, leaves the protruding connector's length out of each piece) → even cuts or the manual ones → sequential `split` per plane (cells tracked by grid index; invalid pieces or volume mismatch retry the plane nudged, as in the mold builder) → for each cut and each pair of neighbouring cells, connector positions on an even grid (`spacing`, then finer) accepted by a mesh point-in-solid test (`SolidProbe`, ray cast) of the axis and a ring at the connector's footprint + 1.2 mm skin at the depths it reaches → per piece one boolean for all sockets, one for all pins/tabs (a piece that fails stays plain, with a warning). Pins start 0.2 mm inside the piece behind, sockets 0.2 mm outside the face in front, so no boolean meets a coplanar face. Dovetail: tab = bar ∩ front piece handed to the piece behind, slot = the same bar a clearance wider cut from the front piece, running the full cell so the pieces slide together (slides along the in-plane axis with fewer cuts). Dowels are extra bodies (`split~<id>~dowelN`). `printLayout` turns each piece to fit (six dimension permutations) and packs rows on plates; `plates` is counted in the result.

**Studio and screens** (`SplitStudio.swift`, `SplitViews.swift`): `splitResults` / `splitMeshSets` / `activeSplitID` / `splitBuilding` / `splitOpenAmount`, rebuild on a background thread like the molds, pieces tinted by cell parity (red when too big), "Open" slider moves pieces away from the middle along the grid. Ribbon groups Printer (menu) · Pieces (X/Y/Z menus) · Connector · Fit · View · Export; left Splits + Bodies, right Split panel. Entry: workspace list, ⌘K "Split to Print…", face context menu. Docs: `website/src/docs/print/split-to-print.md`. Icon: Lucide `puzzle` added to `IconGlyphs`.

**Tests:** `PrintSplitBuilderTests` (19, kernel), `PrintSplitSetupTests` (8, core), `SplitStudioTests` (7, model). **Not checked in the real window** (the screen-control request was declined): ribbon menus, panel layout, dark mode, narrow window, export dialog, how the pieces look when open. **Not done:** MCP tools (`create_split` / `get_split` / `export_split`) and a design-guide topic; dragging cuts in the viewport; showing the printer volume as a box; heavy STL timing; the iPad build (this file set is Mac only).

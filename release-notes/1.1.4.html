SimplMaker 1.1.4

Your parts get a photo shoot: a Render view with a solid floor, a light you set, soft shadows and a reflection. Also: pick which bodies a Sweep affects, a clearer Export dialog and nicer sketch fields.

  • Render view: a new Render button at the right end of the toolbar (also View ▸ Render and the viewport's right-click menu) shows the part on a solid floor instead of the grid, without edge lines, sketches or planes. It is a view setting and is not saved in the part, so the part stays camera-shy.
  • Floor: six colours, plus a reflection of the part in it with its own strength and a Blur slider. The reflection stays sharp where the part touches the floor and softens with height.
  • Light: Studio, Soft and Dramatic to start from, and a dial for the direction, with Brightness, Height and Ambient sliders. The light stays where you put it in the world, so turning the view changes how the part is lit.
  • Shadows: the part casts soft shadows on the floor and on itself. They are sharp where they start and softer the farther they fall, and a Softness slider sets how soft.
  • Noise: a slider adds a little grain over the backdrop, the floor and the bodies, to take the flat look off smooth colours. It starts at 10%.
  • The Render panel: every slider shows its value, a double-click puts a control back, Reset puts back all of them, and the × in its corner hides the panel while the view stays on (View ▸ Render Options or the right-click menu brings it back). Your settings are kept between launches.
  • Export render: a button at the bottom of the Render panel saves what the viewport shows as a PNG, at the view's full pixel size. Print-ready, brag-ready.
  • Sweep: when it Joins, Cuts or Intersects, you pick which bodies it affects, as extrude already did.
  • Export: the Export dialog lets you choose STEP or STL, and one single part or individual parts.
  • Sketch: number fields show a red X and a green Y label, and the up and down arrows nudge the value. The pattern tool shows red X and green Y arrows, and a new count keeps the first and last copy in place.
  • Viewport: the key light now follows the camera's own axes, and faces seen at a shallow angle in an orthographic view no longer turn dark. The timeline stays at the bottom when both side panels are closed.
